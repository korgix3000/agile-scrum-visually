Why Agile: value early, not all at the end

Drag the timeline. Waterfall delivers nothing usable until the very end; Agile ships a working increment every sprint and learns from feedback each time.

Same 40 weeks, two shapes

Waterfall runs each phase once, in order. Agile runs all of them in every two-week sprint, so working software reaches users from the first sprint and the highest-value features come first.

Vangelis Auto ShowThe release plan is 20 two-week sprints. Release 1 (bare-bones Get In app) ships after sprint 7, not after week 40.

10

Story points: size by comparison

Points measure effort, complexity and uncertainty relative to a reference story, not hours. The gaps widen because bigger work is less certain. Click a card.

Planning poker

Everyone picks a card at once, so nobody anchors on the first number said aloud.

From JiraTutorial2Look & Feel spike: 5 points. Neo Core Audio System: 20 points, because design plus development is more complex. Tasks such as the research spike for audio systems get no points.

Why not hours?

People disagree on hours but agree surprisingly well on "about twice as big as that one". Comparing is faster, and it stays true whoever ends up doing the work.

11

Velocity turns points into a forecast

Velocity is the points a team completes per sprint. Click a bar's + or − to change a sprint, then move the backlog slider to see how many sprints remain.

A range, not a promise

Use the slowest and fastest recent sprints to give a range. Velocity settles after 3 or 4 sprints. It's a planning tool for one team, never a target or a way to compare teams.

Increments add up to releases

Drag through the 20 sprints of the release plan. Each sprint adds a working slice to the Get In app; Release 1 and Release 2 (the MVP) bundle them for users.

MVP

A minimum viable product is the smallest version people can actually use and learn from. In the course, Release 1 is bare bones; Release 2 is treated as the MVP because it's the first one with enough features to really try out.
